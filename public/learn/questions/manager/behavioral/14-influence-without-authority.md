# Tell me about a time you influenced without authority

> Critical for senior/staff. You can't just **tell** people what to do — you have to **convince**.

## Strong example

> "Our team was hesitant to adopt automated testing because they felt it slowed delivery. Instead of pushing the practice top-down, I started by adding tests to the **most incident-prone module** and shared the before/after data — incidents in that area dropped, and PR review time actually improved. After a few weeks, other engineers started asking me to help them set up tests in their own areas. Adoption grew naturally without me ever mandating anything."

## Key idea

Influence = **building credibility + showing value + letting people adopt on their own**.

## Recipe

1. **Find a small wedge** — a low-risk, high-visibility area to demonstrate value.
2. **Show data**, not opinions.
3. **Let people pull**, not push. Curiosity beats mandates.
4. **Acknowledge concerns** instead of dismissing them.

## Avoid

- Framing it as "I convinced them I was right".
- Dismissing the team's resistance as ignorance.
- Skipping the part where you actually changed your approach based on their feedback.
