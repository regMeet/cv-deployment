# Tell me about a time you pushed back on scope or a deadline

> They want to see you can **say no constructively** — without being a blocker.

## Strong example

> "We had a release planned with a tight deadline, but during implementation it became clear the original scope would force us to skip critical edge cases — the kind that would generate customer support tickets the day after launch. I went to product with **data on the risk** and proposed two alternatives: ship a smaller scope on the original date, or ship the full scope two weeks later. We agreed on the smaller scope, got the product live on time, and shipped the rest as a follow-up. The launch had **no critical bugs** in the first month."

## Recipe

1. **Don't just say no** — bring options.
2. **Quantify the risk** of the original plan (incidents, support load, tech debt).
3. **Negotiate**, don't dictate. Scope, time, and quality are levers — pick which to flex.
4. **Own the outcome** — once a decision is made, execute it.

## Avoid

- Pushing back without offering alternatives.
- Being vague about why ("it's complicated").
- Making it personal ("product doesn't get it").
