# What engineering practices do you follow?

> Sell yourself: simple, maintainable, observable, business-aware.

## Strong answer

I focus on writing **simple, maintainable, and testable code**, following clear design principles and avoiding unnecessary complexity.

I rely on practices like **code reviews, automated testing, and continuous integration** to ensure quality and consistency.

I also pay close attention to **observability** — logs, metrics, and alerts — so systems are easier to operate and debug in production.

Beyond code, I make sure I understand the **business context**, so I can make better technical decisions and prioritize what really matters.

Overall, I aim to balance **delivery speed with long-term maintainability and system reliability**.

## Why this sounds senior

- "Avoid unnecessary complexity" → judgment, no overengineering.
- CI + testing + reviews → real practices, not theory.
- Observability → you think in production, not just code.
- Business context → impact, not just tech.
- Speed vs maintainability → staff-level mindset.

## Useful technical metrics to mention

System health metrics:

- **Latency** (p95 / p99 response time)
- **Error rate** (failed requests, exceptions)
- **Throughput** (requests per second)
- **Resource usage** (CPU, memory)

> "For backend services, I usually track latency (p95), error rates, and throughput to ensure performance and stability."
