# Tell me about a case where TDD / AI had real impact

> One of the most important questions. Use the **Context → Action → Impact → Learning** structure.

## Strong answer (TDD case)

In one project, we were experiencing **frequent production issues** on a set of critical endpoints, which slowed down development and reduced team confidence.

I introduced **automated tests incrementally**, starting with those high-risk areas, focusing on **integration tests that covered real use cases**.

Over time, this **significantly reduced production incidents** and increased confidence when making changes.

It also **improved our development speed**, since issues were caught earlier in the process instead of after deployment.

## Strong answer (AI case)

More recently, I started leveraging **AI tools** to assist with repetitive tasks like generating boilerplate code, writing initial test cases, and even helping with debugging.

This allowed the team to focus more on **higher-level decisions** like system design and business logic.

We saw improvements in **development speed and iteration time**, especially for new features.

## Make it staff-level

Add concrete metrics or impact (estimates are OK, just credible):

> "We reduced production incidents by around 30% and improved deployment confidence significantly."

> "This helped reduce our debugging time and sped up delivery for new features."

## Always-works structure

1. **Context** — what was the problem?
2. **Action** — what did *you* do? (ownership)
3. **Impact** — what improved? (ideally with metrics)
4. **Learning** — what changed in the team?
