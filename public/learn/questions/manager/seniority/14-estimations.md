# How do you estimate work?

> They're testing **judgment under uncertainty** + communication.

## Strong answer

I try to estimate based on **understanding the problem first**, not just the size of the change.

I break work into smaller pieces and identify the **unknowns** — usually that's where the real risk lives.

For known work, I use historical data or comparison to similar tasks. For uncertain parts, I'd rather **timebox a spike** to reduce ambiguity before committing to a number.

I also communicate estimates as **ranges** when there's real uncertainty (e.g., 3–5 days), not false precision. If I'm given a hard deadline, I'm explicit about what fits and what doesn't.

When I'm wrong, I **flag it early** instead of staying silent and missing the date.

## What sounds senior

- Estimates are about **uncertainty**, not just effort.
- Spikes when needed — don't guess on unknowns.
- Communicate ranges and update them.
- Surface risk early; don't surprise people.
