# How do you handle technical debt?

> They want to see **judgment**, not zealotry. Tech debt is a tool, not a sin.

## Strong answer

I treat tech debt like financial debt — sometimes it's the right move (ship fast, learn, refactor later), and sometimes it's poisoning the team's velocity.

I try to **make debt visible**: tracked in the backlog, tagged, and discussed during planning, instead of just living in people's heads.

When prioritizing, I focus on the debt that **slows the team down or causes incidents**, not just code that "looks ugly".

I also push for **paying it down incrementally** — fixing the area you're already touching, allocating a fixed % of capacity per sprint, or scheduling focused refactors when it makes sense.

The goal is to keep the system **healthy enough to evolve**, without halting feature delivery to chase perfection.

## Signals to convey

- Tech debt = trade-off, not failure.
- Make it **visible and prioritized**, not invisible.
- Pay down where it hurts most (velocity, reliability), not where it's prettiest.
- Balance: never zero debt, never crippling debt.
