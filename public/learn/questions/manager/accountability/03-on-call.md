# Tell me about your on-call experience

> They want to see **operational maturity** + healthy attitude toward incidents.

## Strong answer

On-call has been one of the most **valuable parts of my growth** as an engineer. It forces you to understand systems beyond the code — how they fail, how they recover, and what the user actually feels.

When I'm on-call, I follow a structured approach:

1. **Acknowledge fast** so the team knows it's being handled.
2. **Mitigate first**: rollback, feature flag, traffic shift, isolate the failing component. Restore service before debugging deeply.
3. **Communicate** with stakeholders proactively — status, impact, ETA.
4. After resolution, drive a **blameless postmortem** focused on systemic improvements.
5. Add **monitoring, alerts, or tests** to make recurrence less likely.

I also try to **leave the rotation healthier than I found it**:

- Improve runbooks when I find them lacking.
- Tune or remove noisy alerts that wake people up for nothing.
- Document recurring issues so the next on-call has context.

## Mindset

- **Mitigate before debug.**
- **Comms in parallel**, not after.
- **Blameless postmortems** — focus on the system, not the person.
- Each incident is an opportunity to **invest in resilience**.

## Avoid

- Treating on-call as a chore to survive.
- Heroics over process.
- Skipping the postmortem because "the issue is fixed".
